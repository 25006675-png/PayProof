# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small and medium-sized businesses and their customers use PayProof while settling an order. A payer needs to enter an order, choose SUI or USDC, pay the SME, and leave with evidence that the payment and order match. An SME needs an immediate, independently verifiable record for reconciliation and customer support.

## Product Purpose

PayProof turns a Sui payment into an immediate receipt bound to structured order data. It transfers SUI or USDC to the recipient, records a privacy-preserving hash and essential payment facts on Sui, and produces a detailed receipt that can be downloaded and independently verified. Success means a business can match payment, order, payer, recipient, currency, and transaction without exposing customer or line-item data on a public network.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, clear, efficient. The product should feel composed and practical at the moment money changes hands. Its language is direct, its status feedback is specific, and its visual character comes from a well-kept business ledger rather than crypto spectacle.

## Anti-references

Avoid neon crypto dashboards, trading-terminal density, speculative token imagery, generic corporate banking templates, glass-heavy surfaces, and anonymous “Web3” decoration. Do not make the payment form feel like an exchange or ask users to understand blockchain internals before paying.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the order and payment in one continuous workflow.
2. Explain the consequence of every wallet action before requesting a signature.
3. Put proof within reach: every completed payment produces a readable receipt and a public verification path.
4. Protect commercial privacy by committing order details as a hash instead of publishing customer and line-item data.
5. Treat insufficient funds, rejected signatures, unsupported networks, and pending finality as normal states with clear recovery paths.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard-only operation, visible focus, semantic form structure, screen-reader status announcements, reduced motion, color-blind-safe states, and readable contrast. The first release is responsive across mobile and desktop, uses English interface copy, and displays order values in USD while payments settle in SUI or USDC.
